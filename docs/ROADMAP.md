# Phobicvien Roadmap

This roadmap is a learning and execution guide, not a promise of income. The $10,000 project goal is an ambitious example of a high-value engagement; beginners should build skills, proof, trust, and delivery experience before pursuing projects at that level.

## Phase 1 — Learn the fundamentals

- HTML semantics and accessible page structure.
- CSS layout, responsive design, typography, spacing, and component states.
- JavaScript fundamentals, events, async code, and browser APIs.
- Git/GitHub, command line basics, and debugging with browser devtools.
- Build a personal landing page and a responsive multi-page business site.

**Completion evidence:** projects run locally, work on mobile and desktop, and are published in a portfolio.

## Phase 2 — Build production-minded websites

- Reusable components and a consistent design system.
- Forms with validation, useful error states, and spam protection.
- Backend APIs, databases, authentication, and authorization when needed.
- SEO fundamentals, accessibility checks, performance budgets, and analytics.
- Secure configuration, environment variables, backups, and dependency updates.
- Automated tests for important user journeys.

**Completion evidence:** a deployed project with a README, screenshots, known limitations, and a repeatable setup.

## Phase 3 — Learn discovery and scope

Before estimating a client project, learn:
- Who the customer is and what problem the website solves.
- Required pages, features, integrations, content, and languages.
- Who supplies copy, photography, branding, and product data.
- What counts as done, what is excluded, and how revisions work.
- Hosting, domains, payment processing, maintenance, and ongoing costs.
- Accessibility, privacy, security, and legal requirements relevant to the project.

Write a clear proposal with deliverables, assumptions, milestones, payment schedule, acceptance criteria, and change-request process. Use appropriate adult/professional guidance for contracts and taxes when needed.

## Phase 4 — Build portfolio proof

Create 2–3 realistic case studies, for example:
- A local café or restaurant site with menu, map, and reservation inquiry.
- A service business site with lead form, service pages, and clear calls to action.
- A small catalog or booking prototype with a documented backend.

For every project, show the problem, audience, design decisions, responsive screenshots, core functionality, accessibility/performance checks, and what you would improve. Clearly label fictional/demo clients and do not invent testimonials or results.

## Phase 5 — Deliver real client work

- Start with a narrow, well-defined project.
- Confirm the decision-maker, budget, timeline, content, and dependencies.
- Put scope and responsibilities in writing.
- Use milestone reviews and demonstrate progress frequently.
- Test on real devices and browsers where possible.
- Hand over repository, hosting access, documentation, and ownership clearly.
- Offer maintenance only with a defined service level and scope.

## Phase 6 — Work toward higher-value projects

A high project price usually requires evidence of business value, reliable delivery, specialized capability, trust, and a clear scope. Improve by:
- Specializing in a business niche or measurable outcome.
- Learning conversion, analytics, integrations, and operational workflows.
- Showing honest case studies and asking for permission to share client work.
- Estimating risks, testing, support, and ongoing costs realistically.
- Pricing from project scope and value, not from a guaranteed-income formula.

**Important:** No repository, AI tool, or workflow can guarantee a $10,000 client project or any income level. Treat pricing as a business decision validated with real customers and professional advice.
